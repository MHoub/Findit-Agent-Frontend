[09:46] Result for Question: Which chemical substance is used in wind turbines and causes climate-damaging emissions?

The chemical substance used in wind turbines that causes climate-damaging emissions is sulfur hexafluoride (SF6). It is used in wind turbines’ electrical switchgear as an insulating gas to prevent electric arcs and insulate switches. SF6 has an extremely powerful greenhouse effect and is 22,800 times more potent than carbon dioxide. It takes more than 3,000 years to break down in the atmosphere. (TS_Klimakiller_Schwefelhexafluorid.docx) (PM_Klimakiller_txt_final.docx)

According to the documents, SF6 enters the atmosphere when it leaks from switchgear or is not properly recycled when wind turbines are dismantled. In Germany, Germany is the largest emitter in Europe, and according to scientific studies, actual emissions are significantly higher than the reported data. (TS_Klimakiller_Schwefelhexafluorid.docx) (PM_Klimakiller_txt_final.docx)

Alternatives to SF6 exist, such as the use of vacuum tubes by Siemens Energy. Nevertheless, many manufacturers continue to use SF6 because the costs of alternatives are higher. An EU regulation from 2026 provides for a ban on SF6 in switchgear, but this will not take effect until 2030. (PM_Klimakiller_txt_final.docx)
