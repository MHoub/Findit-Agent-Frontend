[17:19] Result for question: What is Axel Friedrich’s job / role?

Axel Friedrich was head of the "Transport and Noise" department at Germany’s Federal Environment Agency (UBA) for decades and is considered one of the world’s leading experts on automotive exhaust emissions. He played an important role in developing emission standards and has uncovered several emissions scandals since the 1990s. In 2006, he received the "Haagen-Smit Clean Air Award" in recognition of his environmental work. (Abstellkammer.pdf) (FriedrichAbstellkammer.pdf) (EXCL Luft text n2A.doc)

After his retirement in 2007, Axel Friedrich has worked as an independent expert. He systematically measures pollutant emissions from diesel vehicles and makes his results public. His measurements have shown that many vehicles, including those of the latest Euro 6 generation, significantly exceed legal nitrogen oxide limits. (EXCL Luft Schnitt2.doc) (EXCL Luft text v2A.doc) (EXCL Luft Schnitt6.doc)

Friedrich was also directly involved in drafting the EU regulation that introduced the term "Normal Use" to give the automotive industry leeway in complying with emissions rules. He criticized the fact that this provision allowed manufacturers to switch off emission controls under certain conditions. (EXCL Luft text vjA.doc) (ARDExcl KeineLuft text FINAL2Stand3.Juli2017.doc)

Since 2000, he has publicly criticized emissions fraud by the automotive industry, including on German public television (ARD) in 2000. He warned of the health risks posed by nitrogen oxides and called for tighter regulation. He is particularly well known for his work with Deutsche Umwelthilfe (DUH), where he acts as a transport expert. (PM Diesel 1 txt.doc) (Diesel Treatment.docx)

Axel Friedrich has been described in the press as the "father of the catalytic converter" in Germany and is regarded as one of the first people to systematically draw attention to emissions fraud. His earlier work at the UBA and his present role as an independent expert have made him a prominent critic of the automotive industry. (Diesel Treatment.docx) (EXCL Luft Schnitt6.doc)

[21:59] Result for question: What does Axel Friedrich have to do with wood-burning stoves?

For years, Axel Friedrich has been responsible for measuring pollutant emissions from wood-burning stoves and has found that they often cause more air pollution than diesel cars. He has measured air quality in Garmisch-Partenkirchen and other regions, finding that rural areas can sometimes have worse air quality than large cities because of wood stoves. (PM Kaim Kontakte.docx) (PM Kaminöfen txt.docx) (PM Kaminöfen txt. final.docx)

In Berlin’s Papageiensiedlung, Friedrich initiated a pilot project in which 100 old stoves were fitted with soot filters. These filters reduce particulate emissions by more than 95%, significantly improving air quality. The technology uses only 30 watts of power and is less expensive than purchasing new stoves. (PM Kaim Kontakte.docx) (TS Kaminöfen.docx)

According to Germany’s Federal Immission Control Ordinance (BImSchV), stoves installed before 2010 must be replaced by 2024 if they fail to meet pollutant limits. Friedrich points out that modern stoves are often no more effective than older models fitted with retrofit filters, because their real-world emissions can be higher than in laboratory tests. (PM Kaim Kontakte.docx) (TS Kaminöfen.docx)

Friedrich calls for government support for mass production of retrofit filters to reduce costs and protect the environment. He criticizes manufacturers for often preferring to sell new stoves rather than offer affordable retrofit solutions. (PM Kaim Kontakte.docx) (TS Kaminöfen.docx)
