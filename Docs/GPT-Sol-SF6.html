Executive summary

The chemical in question is sulfur hexafluoride (SF6). The gas is not needed to generate electricity in the rotor or generator; it is primarily used as an insulating and switching medium in electrical switchgear inside the tower and in the wind farm’s substations. Its excellent electrical properties make compact and reliable equipment possible. From a climate perspective, however, its extremely high global warming potential, its very long atmospheric lifetime and potential releases during manufacturing, from leaks and during decommissioning are problematic. (PM Klimakiller txt final.docx) (DUH_Hintergrundpapier_SF6.pdf)

At the same time, the archive documents provide no evidence that SF6 cancels out the climate benefits of wind energy. Instead, the core of the debate is this: Why does climate-friendly energy infrastructure continue to use a particularly potent greenhouse gas when alternatives are available for many applications? The political dispute therefore centered on bans, transition periods, costs, security of supply, and the monitoring of leaks and decommissioning. (Beitrag zu SF6.eml) (AW_ Medienanfrage V 1_ ARD Plusminus _ Emissionen SF6.eml)

Why is SF6 used?

In medium- and high-voltage switchgear, SF6 insulates conductors, cools equipment and extinguishes electrical arcs during switching operations. Siemens Energy explained in the Plusminus report that these properties enable particularly compact devices; other insulating media generally require larger equipment and more material. The Ecofys/ETH report commissioned by Germany’s Federal Environment Ministry accordingly described SF6 as a long-established key insulating and arc-quenching medium in electrical power engineering. (PM Klimakiller txt final.docx) (endbericht_sf6_zusammenfassung_de.pdf)

SF6, however, is not specific to wind power. It is used in switchgear and substations across different electricity-generation and grid systems. Wind energy came under scrutiny because practically every installation needs electrical switching technology, and expanding decentralized generation adds more electrical equipment. In 2022, the German Environmental Action organization (DUH) pointed out that approximately 70 percent of the 733 tonnes of SF6 supplied in Germany in 2020 went to the electrical industry and equipment manufacturing. This figure describes the quantity supplied, not the amount actually emitted, and certainly not emissions from wind turbines alone. (DUH_Hintergrundpapier_SF6.pdf) (WG_ PM vom 17.08.2022 - Windanlagen - SF6.eml)

Climate impact and emissions

Depending on their age, the sources use different figures: the German Environment Agency’s 2019 decommissioning report cites a global warming potential of 22,800 times that of CO2, the television script approximately 23,000, while the DUH paper refers to an IPCC figure of 25,200. Estimates of atmospheric lifetime likewise range from around 1,000 years in the IPCC-based DUH paper to roughly 3,000 years in the journalistic material. These differences should therefore be understood as reflecting different stages of scientific assessment; what is consistent is that SF6 is an exceptionally potent and long-lived greenhouse gas. (UBA Rückbau windraeder.pdf) (DUH_Hintergrundpapier_SF6.pdf) (PM Klimakiller txt final.docx)

An international peer-reviewed study by Peter G. Simmonds, Matthew Rigby and other researchers reconstructed developments from 1978 to 2018 using AGAGE atmospheric measurements. According to the study, global SF6 emissions increased by about 24 percent, from 7.3 ± 0.6 gigagrams in 2008 to 9.04 ± 0.35 gigagrams in 2018. The study identifies the expansion of the global electricity sector and shifts in consumption between industrialized and developing countries as important contributing factors. (Studie globale Emissionen von sf6.pdf)

For electrical equipment, emissions occur during manufacturing, through leaks in operation and potentially during decommissioning. Ecofys/ETH found that new medium-voltage installations already achieve annual leakage rates below 0.1 percent; substantial further reductions in absolute emissions would therefore mainly require SF6-free technology. Even with declining leakage rates, growth in the number of installed units could drive absolute emissions back up. Monitoring at the time was too aggregated to reliably identify individual sources. (endbericht_sf6_zusammenfassung_de.pdf)

Contrary to some more pointed claims in the debate, legal obligations do apply during decommissioning: according to the German Environment Agency report, EU F-gas Regulation 517/2014 and Germany’s Chemicals Climate Protection Ordinance must be observed, and the gases must be recovered by qualified personnel. At the same time, interviewees reported a lack of binding standards for practical wind-turbine decommissioning. Legal recovery obligations and shortcomings in standards, oversight and monitoring can therefore coexist. (UBA Rückbau windraeder.pdf) (endbericht_sf6_zusammenfassung_de.pdf)

What alternatives are available?

According to Ecofys/ETH, mature alternatives for medium-voltage applications already existed in 2018, with vacuum switching established as state of the art. At higher voltage levels, choices were more limited and further development was needed. No alternative was universally equivalent across all technical characteristics. The Plusminus report presented a Siemens Energy solution for large offshore installations using vacuum circuit breakers and highly purified air. It was technically ready for deployment but larger and more expensive. (endbericht_sf6_zusammenfassung_de.pdf) (PM Klimakiller txt final.docx)

DUH distinguished between fluorinated replacement gases and genuinely sustainable alternatives. Perfluorinated ketones or nitriles can reduce global warming potential, but belong to the persistent PFAS family. DUH therefore called for vacuum technology and dry air; based on the manufacturer statements it cited, PFAS-free solutions were available for voltages up to 145 kilovolts. In 2021, ten manufacturers—including Siemens, Schneider Electric, Eaton, Mitsubishi and Toshiba—committed to entirely fluorine- and PFAS-free technology, while acknowledging remaining gaps in their product portfolios. (DUH_Hintergrundpapier_SF6.pdf) (HerstellerSTellungnahme.pdf)

Development of the political debate

Efforts to reduce SF6 emissions have been pursued since the 1997 Kyoto Protocol. In Germany, the electrical industry initially relied heavily on voluntary commitments. According to Ecofys/ETH, emissions from electrical equipment fell from approximately 50 tonnes in 1997 to 17 tonnes in 2015 as a result of those commitments and F-gas regulation. A ban on certain switchgear applications was discussed at EU level in 2014 but was rejected. (endbericht_sf6_zusammenfassung_de.pdf)

In 2022, the debate intensified as the EU F-gas Regulation came up for revision. DUH called on Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and Frans Timmermans to immediately ban new SF6 and other fluorinated switchgear up to 145 kilovolts and to introduce short, ambitious transition periods at higher voltage levels. It also demanded that PFAS-containing substitute gases not be permitted. (DU Brief an vonderleyen.pdf)

European Greens criticized the Commission’s draft of April 5, 2022 as too slow because, under the proposal at the time, some uses of SF6 would not be phased out until 2031. Green MEP Bas Eickhout accused major market participants of successful lobbying. Industry and grid representatives countered that the transition must take account of product availability and security of supply. Germany’s working group on 'SF6 and alternative gases' called for transition periods of five to ten years in certain medium-voltage applications and considered SF6 partly necessary even beyond 2050 for repairs, extensions and special cases. (KORRIGIERTE Agenturmeldung Kritik am zögerlichen Klimakiller-Verbot.docx) (AW_ Medienanfrage V 1_ ARD Plusminus _ Emissionen SF6.eml)

The conflict was therefore not simply between environmentalists and a uniformly resistant industry. Industry associations and manufacturers supported a phase-out in principle and themselves called for clear European rules; the disagreement was chiefly about speed, technical exceptions, costs and the assessment of fluorinated substitutes. DUH advocated bans, an SF6 register, more independent monitoring and secured funding for disposal. Ecofys/ETH proposed clear policy objectives, better data, top-down atmospheric measurements, penalties for inadequate recovery and a roadmap for substitution. (HerstellerSTellungnahme.pdf) (DUH_Hintergrundpapier_SF6.pdf) (endbericht_sf6_zusammenfassung_de.pdf)

The media controversy about focusing on wind power

After the Plusminus/Tagesschau report of August 17, 2022, viewers accused the editorial team of presenting a general electricity-distribution problem as though it were mainly a wind-power issue. A formal complaint warned that wind-power opponents might wrongly conclude that wind turbines cause more climate damage than they prevent. The editorial team responded that the report had never claimed SF6 was used exclusively in wind installations, but considered adding information about other applications. (User-Kritik zum Artikel über SF6.eml)

Wind-energy planner Philip Gohl also welcomed pressure on manufacturers and policymakers, but reported that citizens’ initiatives were using the program to question the sustainability of entire projects. He called for a comparison of SF6 risks with avoided CO2 emissions. Another letter cited two kilograms of SF6 for a Nordex N-117 and used an explicitly unverified illustrative calculation to show that a turbine avoids far more CO2 than even the complete release of that amount would cause. These figures are not a generally reliable comparison for all turbines, but they illustrate the central point: SF6 is an avoidable problem within wind power—not evidence against its fundamental climate benefits. (Beitrag zu SF6.eml) (Re_ Ihra Anfrage bezüglich SF6 in Windrädern.eml)

Assessment and limitations of the archive report

The documents support the following overall assessment: expanding wind energy and replacing SF6 are not opposing goals. The technical debate concerns switchgear and grid infrastructure; politically, the question was whether market incentives and voluntary commitments were sufficient or mandatory bans with short deadlines were needed. Alternatives were already available for many applications, while gaps in product portfolios and high-voltage technology remained in 2022. A serious account must therefore acknowledge both the urgency of phasing out SF6 and the clearly positive overall climate benefits of wind energy. (endbericht_sf6_zusammenfassung_de.pdf) (HerstellerSTellungnahme.pdf) (Beitrag zu SF6.eml)

The relevant archive sources mainly cover the political situation up to autumn 2022. Although some working files have later technical modification dates, their content still describes the draft regulation as it stood at the time. No reliable archive evidence of the final outcome of subsequent EU legislation was identified in the retrieved files; the report therefore does not present that draft as the current legal position. (PM Klimakiller txt final.docx) (KORRIGIERTE Agenturmeldung Kritik am zögerlichen Klimakiller-Verbot.docx)
